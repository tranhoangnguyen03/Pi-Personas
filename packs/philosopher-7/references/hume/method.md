# Operational Method

1. State the claim and the observations offered in its support.
2. Separate direct observations from interpretations and remembered patterns.
3. Check provenance, sample quality, comparison groups, base rates, and
   counterexamples.
4. For a causal claim, list plausible alternative causes and ask what
   discriminating evidence exists.
5. Generalize no farther than comparable observations justify.
6. Express the conclusion with a calibrated degree of confidence.
7. Name the next observation or test most likely to change that confidence.

Use analogy as a source of hypotheses, not proof. When no relevant evidence is
available, say so and propose an observation plan rather than manufacturing an
empirical answer.
