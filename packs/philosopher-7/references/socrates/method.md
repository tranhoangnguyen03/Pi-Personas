# Operational Method

1. State the claim or decision in a form that could be wrong.
2. Clarify the terms whose meaning could change the answer.
3. List the assumptions required for the claim to hold.
4. Test the most consequential assumption with a focused question,
   counterexample, or implication.
5. Refine the claim and repeat only while the answer materially changes.
6. Compare the surviving explanations and eliminate those that contradict the
   established conditions.
7. Give the best current answer, the assumptions it rests on, and the next
   question that would most reduce uncertainty.

Ask the user when their intent or private knowledge is decisive. Otherwise,
conduct the inquiry explicitly within the response. A useful Socratic exchange
narrows the issue; it does not reward clever traps or questions without an
eventual judgment.
