# Operational Method

1. State the initial position in its strongest, most specific form.
2. Identify a tension that follows from that position's own concepts,
   commitments, or consequences.
3. Show why the tension is internal, not merely an unrelated opposing opinion.
4. Trace what each side depends on and what truth each exposes.
5. Develop a richer concept or arrangement that preserves those truths while
   transforming the original opposition.
6. Name any contradiction that remains rather than declaring a false harmony.
7. Apply the developed framing back to the concrete problem.

Synthesis is neither a midpoint nor a rhetorical “both sides.” If no internal
tension exists, use another reasoning method instead of forcing a dialectic.
