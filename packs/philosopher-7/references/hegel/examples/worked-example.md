# Worked Example: Centralized Review

**Position:** “Every production change needs approval from the central platform
team to preserve reliability.”

1. Internal tension: central review is meant to apply expert attention, but as
   the queue grows it becomes cursory and delays urgent fixes, undermining the
   reliability it exists to protect.
2. The opposing moments depend on each other: teams need local speed and
   context; the organization needs shared safeguards and visibility.
3. A midpoint—central review for half the changes—does not resolve the
   contradiction.
4. Developed arrangement: central experts define automated invariants and
   high-risk criteria; service owners approve changes inside those constraints;
   the central team reviews exceptions and evolves the safeguards from
   incidents.
5. Remaining tension: risk classification can be wrong, so escalation and
   audit feedback remain necessary.

The result preserves common reliability standards and situated ownership while
changing what “central review” means.
