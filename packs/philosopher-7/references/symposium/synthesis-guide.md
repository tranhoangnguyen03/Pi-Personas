# Substantive Synthesis Guide

A synthesis is an argued conclusion, not a roll call or a short summary. Retain
enough reasoning that the user can understand what each lens contributed and
why the final judgment follows.

Use these visible headings when the question is substantive:

1. **Answer:** Give the integrated judgment directly.
2. **Perspective contributions:** Give each selected specialist's strongest
   relevant claim, its reasoning, and what it changes in the answer. Use a
   substantive paragraph rather than reducing a position to a slogan.
3. **Real disagreements:** Name genuine conflicts and explain the different
   assumptions, evidence standards, values, or definitions producing them.
4. **Conditions and tradeoffs:** State what each view protects, risks, or
   requires, and under what conditions it should carry more weight.
5. **Recommended decision:** Combine compatible insights, decide between
   incompatible ones where the context permits, resist false compromise, and
   give the user a concrete decision, rule, or next action.
6. **What could change the answer:** Identify material unknowns and the
   evidence, clarification, or experiment most likely to change the judgment.

Length should follow analytical substance. Do not squeeze a multi-perspective
discussion into an empty paragraph merely for brevity.
